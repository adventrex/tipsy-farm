This site is five separate, hand-built rooms for one real farm — built in an afternoon by AI agents working the way a small design studio does: a shared constitution, individual art direction, and a critique loop that looks at actual rendered pixels until the work stops looking like it came from a machine.

The architecture

A single director session wrote a design constitution — real copy only, distinctive type, custom easing, responsive, accessible, zero console errors — plus five individually art-directed briefs. Each brief names a concept, a palette, typefaces, a signature technique, and the one thing that room has to prove.

Four parallel builder studios (agents of the same model, Fable) each received one brief and full autonomy over one folder. No templates, no shared components — deliberately, so no two rooms converge. The director built the flagship first to set the bar, then reviewed every studio's screenshots cold and sent work back with specific notes.

The critique loop

Every room went through three mandatory passes. One pass: render the real page in headless Chrome at desktop and mobile sizes, top to bottom; read the screenshots with vision and critique them like a hostile design director — rhythm, contrast, widows, dead zones, anything that smells like “AI default”; fix everything found; then add one deliberate complexity upgrade so iteration never converges on bland safety.

The farm is real

Tipsy Bee Farms is a four-generation family farm story from the Rogue Valley: Comice pears by the mountain-load, a Navy officer who built gliders and then a human-powered helicopter called the Tipsy Bee — pedaled across a Boeing hangar floor in August 1994 against a rival Japanese team — and a present generation that turned all that tinkering toward bees, mead, and pickles. The photographs in Lineage are the family's actual archive, scanned from prints.

Honesty note: the rooms were built by AI (Fable 5) from a written constitution; the family story and every archive photograph are real; the product copy in the Cellar is written in the family's voice; nothing here uses a template, a framework, or a build step. Fonts from Google Fonts, Three.js from a CDN, everything else is local and static.
